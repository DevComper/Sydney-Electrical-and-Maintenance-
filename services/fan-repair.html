How We Diagnose Fan Problems

We use a simple, methodical approach to identify the cause of fan faults quickly.

  • Visual & Mounting Check: Inspect canopy and mounting for loose hardware.
  • Blade Balance: Measure and correct blade imbalance to remove wobble.
  • Electrical Test: Check capacitors, switches and wiring for faults.
  • Motor Assessment: Determine if motor replacement or full fan replacement is required.

Most repairs are completed on the spot; if we recommend replacement, we provide clear options and pricing.

When Repair Isn't Enough

Some fan faults are best fixed; others mean replacement is safer and more cost-effective. We help you choose the right option.

  • Seized or Noisy Motor: If the motor is burnt or makes grinding noises, replacement is usually required.
  • Repeated Electrical Failures: Persistent capacitor or wiring faults indicate underlying issues that may warrant a full unit replacement.
  • Damaged Mounting or Structural Issues: Bent brackets, damaged joists or unsafe mounting require replacement and proper reinforcement.
  • Obsolete or Unsafe Models: Very old fans may lack modern safety features and are better replaced.
  • Cost vs Reliability: When repair costs approach the price of a new, energy-efficient fan, replacement is often the smarter choice.

How to Prevent Fan Problems

  • Inspect mounting points after the first month and annually thereafter.
  • Keep blades clean to maintain balance and airflow.
  • Replace capacitors at the first sign of intermittent starting.
  • Ensure remotes and speed controls are correctly paired and configured.
  • Book a professional service if you notice unusual noises or wobble.
